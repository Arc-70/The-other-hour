## Chapter 54: The Veil

The Atrium was empty.

It was eleven o'clock at night, and the long dark hall with the peacock-blue ceiling was empty from end to end, and the fireplaces were cold, and the golden fountain was still going in the middle of it all, the wizard and the witch and the centaur and the goblin and the house-elf, with the water falling from their wands and arrow-tips and hats and ears into the pool, and nobody to hear it.

There was no security guard at the desk.

Harry noticed it. He did not say anything. He read the empty desk and the empty hall, and the second telephone box coming down behind him with Ron and Hermione and Millicent in it, and the lifts at the far end, waiting, with their golden grilles open.

"Where's the guard?" said Hermione, coming out of the box.

"Gone," said Harry. "Somebody wanted us to get in."

Nobody said anything after that.

They went down the hall past the fountain. Their footsteps were very loud on the polished floor. The house-elf in the fountain looked up at the golden wizard with its ears pouring water, adoring, and Harry did not look at it.

They took the lift to the bottom.

* * *

He knew the way.

That was the strange thing, the thing that made the others look at him in the dark corridor with the black stone walls. He did not hesitate. He went down the corridor from the lift without slowing down, past the turning to the stairs where he had gone down to Courtroom Ten in August, and stopped at the plain black door at the end, and put his hand flat on it.

It opened.

The room beyond it was round. Everything in it was black, the floor and the ceiling and the walls, and set into the walls at equal distances there were doors, a dozen of them, black and handleless and identical. Candles with blue flames stood in brackets between them. Their light shone on the marble floor so that it looked like dark water.

"Shut the door," said Harry.

Neville shut it.

The wall began to turn.

It turned fast, with a great rumbling, and the blue candles streaked into lines of light, and then it stopped, and every door looked exactly as it had before, and there was no way of telling which one they had come in by.

"Oh," said Luna, interested.

"Which one?" said Ron.

Harry did not answer at once.

He had been here. He had stood in this room at thirteen, in another world, with Evelyn's hand in his, and he had felt it then the way he felt it now. Behind every door there was a different kind of quiet. Behind one there was the quiet of a great many small glass things, all faintly humming. Behind another there was the quiet of a pool. Behind another, a great many things that ticked.

And behind one there was the cold.

It was very large. It was very still. It was a long way down, at the bottom of a room shaped like a bowl, and it was waiting, and Harry felt it know he was here.

He did not go to that door.

He went to the one with the humming.

"This one," he said.

* * *

The Hall of Prophecy was as high as a church and full of shelves.

They went away into the dark on both sides, towering, covered in small dusty glass spheres that glowed faintly in the light of more candles, blue and silver and grey. There were thousands of them. There were tens of thousands. Each one sat on a little label, and under each label there was a number, and the rows were numbered in brass at the ends.

"Ninety-seven," said Harry.

"How do you know?" said Ron.

"I just do."

He went down the aisle between the shelves. He did not run. The others came after him, close together, wands out, Evelyn on his left with her shoulder very near his arm. He counted the rows. Fifty-three. Fifty-four. He could hear them all breathing.

Row ninety-seven.

He turned into it.

There was nobody on the floor. There was no Sirius, lying on his back at the end of the aisle with his arms out. There had never been. He had known there would not be. He had known since a hot Friday afternoon in the Great Hall with the sun on the back of his neck, and he had known for two years before that, since a night in another world.

He walked down the aisle anyway.

He knew which one. He did not need to read the labels. He went down the row until he was about two-thirds of the way along, and stopped, and looked up at a small glass sphere on the shelf at the height of his eyes, glowing with a dull inner light, very dusty.

The label under it was in spidery writing and had yellowed.

*S.P.T. to A.P.W.B.D. Dark Lord and (?) Harry Potter.*

"Harry," said Hermione, very quietly. "That's got your name on it."

"I know."

"Don't touch it."

He reached up and took it off the shelf.

It was cool in his hand. It was the size of a Snitch. There was something moving inside it, grey and slow, like smoke in a closed jar.

"*Very* good, Potter," said a cold drawling voice behind him. "Now turn round, nice and slowly, and give that to me."

* * *

They came out of the dark on both sides.

Black shapes. Hooded, masked, wands lit. They came out of the aisles to the left and the right and stood at both ends of row ninety-seven, and there were twelve of them, and Harry read them all at once, in one breath, the way he had read the Wizengamot.

They were excited. That was the top of all of them. They had been waiting here for some time, in the dark, between the shelves, and now the boy had come, as they had been told he would, and the waiting was over.

Under the excitement, most of them were frightened. Not of Harry. Of somebody who was not in this room.

And one of them was laughing.

Harry knew that laugh. He had read it in a photograph on the front of the *Prophet* in January, among ten others, an amusement so private and so enormous that it had seemed to come off the paper. It was standing at the far end of the row, beside the drawling voice, and it was laughing at him now, quietly, from behind its mask.

"Give it to me, Potter," said Lucius Malfoy.

He had taken off his mask. He had pale hair to his shoulders and a pale pointed face, and he was holding out his hand, and he was smiling.

Harry read him.

He was eager. He was very, very eager, and it was aimed at the glass sphere in Harry's hand. And under the eagerness, quite large, quite close to the top, there was fear. None of it was for Harry. It went downward, through the floor, at a round room at the bottom of the bowl. Lucius Malfoy had been told to fetch this sphere. He had been told what would happen if he did not.

"Do you know what this is?" said Harry.

"It is your prophecy," said Lucius. "It is the reason the Dark Lord came for you, fifteen years ago. It is the reason for everything that has happened to you since. It is the only thing in this building that can tell you *why*, Potter." He tilted his head. "Don't you want to know?"

"I know what it says."

Lucius's smile did not move. But something in him did.

"You cannot," he said.

"I know what it says," said Harry. "Born as the seventh month dies. Marked as his equal. A power he doesn't know. Neither can live while the other survives." He looked at the small cool sphere in his hand. "I've heard it. Somewhere you'll never go."

Somebody at the far end laughed. It was a woman's laugh, high and delighted.

"Ooh," said Bellatrix Lestrange, behind her mask. "Ooh, he's been *listening*, Lucius. Ickle baby Potter's been listening at doors."

"Be quiet, Bella."

"He knows what it says! He says he *knows*!" She laughed again. "Shall we see if he's telling the truth? Shall I ask him nicely? I can ask *very* nicely, Potter. Ask the Longbottoms."

Behind Harry, Neville made a sound.

It was not a word. Harry read him make it. The hate in him, the new quiet hate from January, came up all at once to the very top of him, and it was so large and so steady that for a moment it was the only thing in the row.

"Neville," said Harry. "Not yet."

"*Longbottom?*" said Bellatrix. She leaned forward. "Is that little Longbottom? Frank's boy? Oh, how is your *father*, Longbottom? Does he still…"

"Bella," said Lucius, very sharply.

He read Lucius go very still.

No anger. Not even surprise. Only the feeling of a man who has been told to bring a particular thing to a particular person, and who understands, standing at the end of a row of shelves in the dark, that the particular thing is about to stop existing.

"Potter," said Lucius. "Don't…"

Harry opened his hand.

* * *

It fell.

He did not throw it. He let it go, and it fell from his fingers onto the stone floor of the Hall of Prophecy and smashed, very quietly, like a light bulb dropped on a carpet.

There was a figure in the smoke.

It rose out of the broken glass, pearly white, ghostly, a woman wrapped in shawls with enormous magnified eyes behind enormous spectacles. Her mouth moved. She was speaking. Harry could see her lips shaping the words he had heard once in another world, in a voice like a cracked bell.

But in the Hall, with twelve people shouting at once, nobody heard a word.

Then the figure dissolved into the air and was gone.

Harry looked at Lucius Malfoy.

He would remember Lucius's face for a long time. He would remember it on a great many nights, the same way he remembered a woman's terror in a forest clearing, and he would not be sure, any more than he was sure about the clearing, how much of what he felt about it was right. It was not a face of rage. Rage would have been easier. It was the face of a man who has just watched the only thing that could have saved him smash on a floor, and who is thinking, very clearly and very fast, about who is waiting for him downstairs.

Under the face, Harry read terror.

"Now nobody knows," said Harry. "Except me."

Then everything happened at once.

* * *

"*STUPEFY!*"

It was Millicent. She did not wait for anybody to tell her. She was at the back of the group, facing the far end of the row, and she sent a Stunner straight down the aisle at the nearest masked figure and hit him in the chest, and he went over backwards into a shelf.

The shelf came down.

It came down slowly at first, and then fast, and it hit the shelf beside it, and that one went too, and suddenly there were glass spheres falling everywhere, hundreds of them, smashing on the floor like hail, and out of every one of them rose a pale figure, speaking, and nobody could hear any of them.

"*RUN!*" Harry shouted.

They ran.

He did not remember all of it afterwards. He remembered pieces. He remembered Hermione shouting *Reducto* at the shelves on either side so that they came down between the Death Eaters and the others. He remembered Ron taking a Stunner on his shoulder and going down and Millicent picking him up by the collar of his robes, without stopping, and dragging him. He remembered Luna, quite calm, saying *Stupefy* at a man who had grabbed Neville round the neck, and the man falling, and Neville turning round and staring at her.

He remembered Evelyn.

She was beside him the whole way, on his left. She did not cast anything. She did not run any faster than she had to. She ran beside him through the falling glass with her wand out and her face perfectly still, and every few seconds she said something in a flat voice, very fast, and it was always useful.

"Left."

"Two behind you."

"Not that door. That's the one we came in."

He read the room as they ran. He could not stop himself. It was enormous and loud and full of things all at once, the Death Eaters' excitement turning to anger, the others' terror, Ron's pain, Neville's determination, which was like a lit fuse. And under all of it, under the whole Hall and the whole Department, a long way down at the bottom of the chamber, the cold.

It had not moved. It did not hurry. It waited.

They came out of the Hall of Prophecy through a door at the far end and into a room full of clocks.

* * *

He had been in the room full of clocks before.

He had come through it at thirteen, with Evelyn, to wind a watch. It was full of light and ticking, and there was a great bell jar at the end of it with a glittering wind in it and an egg that hatched into a bird and became an egg again, over and over. Clocks on every surface, on the walls and the desks and the floor. The noise of them was like rain on a roof.

They went through it at a run with the Death Eaters behind them, and somebody hit the bell jar with a curse, and it did not break, and the head of the man who had cast it turned into a baby's head on a man's body, and he screamed in a baby's voice, and Harry did not stop to look.

They came out into the circular room.

The doors began to turn.

"*Which one?*" shouted Neville.

The wall stopped.

And Harry felt, through the floor, through the stone, from somewhere very close and very deep, a door open. Not a door in the wall. A door in the cold.

It was the Marks.

He felt them burn. Not his. Theirs. Every Death Eater in the Department, the ones behind them in the clock room and the ones scattered through the Hall of Prophecy and the ones he had not seen, all at once, at the same moment, felt a summons go through the black skulls on their arms. He felt it in all of them, the way he had felt the whole school laugh. It was one feeling, the same in every one of them, and it was the feeling of a dog that has heard its name.

*Come.*

They stopped chasing.

He felt them stop. He felt them turn, all of them, everywhere in the Department, away from the clock room and the Hall of Prophecy, and go. They went fast. They went towards one door in the circular room that Harry had not chosen, and through it, and down.

Down into the bowl.

"They've gone," said Hermione. She was breathing very hard. "Harry, they've… why have they gone?"

Harry did not answer.

He was looking at the door they had gone through. It was a black door like the others, handleless and identical. He did not need to read what was behind it. He had felt it since the telephone box. He had felt it for six weeks.

The door on the other side of the circular room opened.

Not one of theirs: the one they had come in by, from the corridor. And through it, very fast, wands lit, came Sirius Black and Remus Lupin and Nymphadora Tonks and Alastor Moody and Kingsley Shacklebolt.

* * *

"*Harry!*"

Sirius had him by both shoulders. He was looking at him all over, very fast, the way you look at somebody you have been terrified for, checking every part of them at once.

"I'm fine," said Harry. "I'm fine. Ron's hurt. His shoulder."

"Where are they?"

"Gone. Down. Through there." Harry pointed at the door. "He called them. He's down there. He's been down there all night."

He read the Order as they came in. Sirius's terror for him, turning to relief so fast and so hard that it was nearly anger. Lupin, grey and quiet. Kingsley, the still river, very calm, very fast. Moody, afraid of everything, as always, and under it the floor of him, steady.

Tonks was grinning.

She had her hair pink again. She was out of breath and there was a cut on her cheek, and she was grinning at him, and she was frightened, and she was happy. She was happy all the way down, even now. She was happy to be here, with these people, in this room, doing this.

"Wotcher, Harry," she said.

"Tonks."

"Dumbledore?" said Moody.

"Coming," said Kingsley. "As fast as he can. He sent word at half past ten. He was in Albania."

"Then we hold till he gets here," said Moody. "We don't go down. We don't go near that arch. We hold the door, and we wait for Albus, and if anybody comes up through it we…"

"He's going to start," said Harry.

They looked at him.

"Now," said Harry. "He's going to start now. He's been waiting for me. He knew I'd come. He made me come." He looked at the black door. "He doesn't need to come up. He needs me to go down."

"Then you don't go down," said Lupin.

"I know," said Harry.

He read the cold, at the bottom of the bowl.

He read it begin.

* * *

It was not loud.

That was the first thing. He had expected it to be loud. He had felt the cold in a churchyard, enormous, filling the night. He had felt it through his scar every night for six weeks, laying things out on a table.

This was quiet.

It was the quiet of a man reaching out his hand.

And at the bottom of the chamber, on the stone steps, five small cold hungers felt it reach for them. And answered.

Harry was through the door before anybody could stop him.

He did not decide. He did not think. He was standing in the circular room with Sirius's hand on his shoulder and then he was through the black door and on the top step of a long stone stair going down, and Sirius was shouting behind him, and Evelyn was beside him, on his left, because she was always on his left, and she had not been told to come and she had come.

He looked down.

* * *

The Death Chamber was shaped like a bowl.

It was very large and very dim, and it was made of stone, and its sides went down in long shallow steps, like the benches in Courtroom Ten, all the way round, to a sunken floor at the bottom. And in the middle of the floor there was a raised dais, and on the dais there was an archway.

It was very old. It was cracked and crumbling. It stood on its own with nothing holding it up, and there was no wall behind it, and in it there was a black curtain, tattered, like a veil.

The veil was moving.

There was no wind. It was moving all the same, very slightly, rippling, as though somebody had touched it from the other side.

Harry had watched a man fall through it.

He stood on the top step and looked at it, and he heard, behind it, very faint, the sound he had heard the first time, in another world. Voices. Whispering. A great many of them, very quiet, very far away. He had never known what they were saying. He did not know now.

And in front of the arch, on the dais, there was a man.

* * *

He was tall and very thin, and he was wearing black robes, and his face was whiter than a skull. He had no nose. He had slits for nostrils, like a snake, and his eyes were red, and his long white hands were held a little way out from his sides, palms up, the way a man holds his hands when he has just put something down.

The Death Eaters were all round him.

They were standing on the steps of the bowl, in a ring, thirty of them, forty, masked and hooded, with their wands out. They were not looking up at the door where Harry stood. They were looking at the man on the dais. All of them. The way the house-elf in the fountain looked at the golden wizard.

And on the steps below the dais, laid out in a curve, with a little space between each of them, there were four things.

A gold locket, heavy, on a long chain, with an S set into its lid in small green stones.

A ring. Gold, heavy, ugly, with a black stone in it.

A small golden cup with two handles and a badger engraved on its side.

And a tarnished diadem, with a blue stone in the front of it, and words engraved round the band.

At the man's feet, coiled on the dais, there was a snake.

It was enormous. It was twelve feet long and as thick as a man's thigh, and its scales were dark green, and it lay in loose heavy coils round the bottom of the arch with its head raised, looking up at the man on the dais.

Harry read them.

The locket was hungry. The ring was hungry. The cup was hungry. The diadem was hungry, the same hunger he had felt in a cathedral of hidden things, thin and cold and endless. The snake was hungry too, underneath the snake. Under the cold blank animal quiet he could not read, the way he could not read any snake, there was the same hunger, small and cold, curled up inside it.

Five.

And the compass needle at the bottom of Harry turned towards them all at once, and pulled.

Lord Voldemort looked up at the top of the steps.

He looked at Harry. He looked at him across the whole of the Death Chamber, past forty Death Eaters and four hungry things and a snake, with his red eyes, and he smiled.

He opened his arms.

"Harry," he said. "I am so glad you came."

* * *

"Get *back*!"

Sirius was behind him. Sirius had him by the arm and was pulling, and the Order was coming through the door, and Moody was shouting, and spells were coming up the steps from the Death Eaters at the bottom, red and green and purple, very fast. Harry was pulled back against the wall. Kingsley was beside him with a shield up. Lupin. Tonks. Ron and Hermione and Millicent and Neville and Luna were coming through the door behind them, and Evelyn was still on his left.

"Hold the top!" shouted Moody. "Hold the top of the stairs! Don't go down! Nobody goes down!"

They held the top.

"Neville, left!"

"I see him!"

"Ron, get *down*…"

"Kingsley, the big one, he's going round…"

"Got him!"

"Luna, behind you!"

"Thank you," said Luna, and Stunned the man behind her without looking round.

It was very loud. It was the loudest thing Harry had ever been in. It was spells hitting stone and stone breaking and people shouting, forty of them below and twelve of them above, and the dim light of the Death Chamber lit up red and green, over and over, like lightning in a cloud.

Harry did not cast anything.

He stood against the wall at the top of the steps, behind Kingsley's shield, and he looked at the man on the dais. Voldemort had not moved. He was not fighting. He was standing in front of the arch with his arms still a little open and his face turned up, and he was not looking at Harry any more. He was not looking at anything. He had shut his eyes.

He was reaching.

Harry felt him reach.

He felt it the way he had felt it in the circular room, quiet, a man holding out his hand. He felt it go out of the man on the dais in five directions at once, very gentle, towards five small cold hungers on the steps around him.

*Come home.*

He did not say it. There were no words in it. Harry felt it anyway, and that was what it was. No command in it, and no Dominion. Something gentler than that, and much worse. It was a man calling to the pieces of himself.

The locket cracked.

It made a sound like a stone dropped on ice. A thin black line went across the green S on its lid, and something came out of the line. It was dark. It was not smoke, and it was not shadow, and it was not anything Harry had a name for. It rose out of the locket and went across the space between the steps and the dais, and into the man standing there, the way water goes into sand.

The ring cracked.

The black stone did not break. The gold band split, with the same small sound, and the darkness came out of the split and went into Voldemort.

The cup.

The diadem. The blue stone in the front of it went dull, all at once, and a long crack went round the band through the engraved words, and the darkness came out, a thin cold thread of it, and went home.

The snake.

It did not crack. It went rigid, all twelve feet of it, at once, with its head thrown back, and its mouth opened wide, and something came out of its mouth, dark, and went up into the man above it. Then the snake fell back into its coils on the dais, and lay there, and was a snake.

Harry read it.

The hunger in it was gone. There was nothing under the blank animal quiet any more. It was only a snake. A very large and very dangerous snake, coiled on the floor at the bottom of a stone bowl, with nothing inside it but itself.

Voldemort opened his eyes.

He looked up the steps.

He looked at Harry.

And he called the last one.

* * *

It did not come from outside.

That was what Harry would remember. Everything else had come from outside. The locket and the ring and the cup and the diadem and the snake, all of them out there on the steps, below him, a long way off. He had felt them answer the way you hear somebody answer from another room.

This answered from inside him.

It was the compass needle. It was the small cold thing at the bottom of him that had turned towards the diadem in a cathedral of hidden things, and that he had felt in the summer at Privet Drive, and in himself, once, after another world, and called a stain. It was the thing he had felt in a boy in a red and gold scarf, at the very bottom of him, and been sick.

It turned.

It turned all the way round, towards the man on the dais, and it pulled.

And it was not small.

It had never been small. It had only been deep. It was so deep that he had never found the bottom of it, because it did not have a bottom. It went all the way down through him. It went down through every floor of him, through every room, under all of it, under the cupboard and the giant and the train, under Quirrell and the diary and the study, under the boy at the window in the hospital wing with his face wet. It was everywhere. It had been everywhere all his life, so quiet and so cold and so deep that he had thought it was the floor.

It began to come out.

* * *

His scar split.

He felt it go. The churchyard had hurt, and the nights had hurt, and this was nothing like either. This was like being opened. The skin on his forehead parted along the old line, and there was blood in his eyes, and he was on his knees on the top step of the Death Chamber with his hands over his face, and he could not see.

Something was being pulled out of him.

He could feel it go. He could feel it come up through all the floors of him at once, the cold thing, the deep thing, the thing he had thought was the floor, and go out through the split in his forehead, and he could feel the places where it had been. They were not empty. They were open. Every place in him where it had been was open, suddenly, for the first time, the way a room is open when a wall you had not known was a wall comes down.

He opened his eyes.

He could see through the blood. He could see the dais, a long way below. He could see Voldemort standing in front of the arch with his face turned up and his arms open, and a thin cold line of darkness coming down the steps towards him from the top, from Harry, from the split in his forehead, going home.

And he could see the snake.

It was moving.

It had uncoiled itself from the dais. It was coming up the steps. It was coming up very fast, the way snakes move, long and dark and silent, between the duelling Death Eaters and the Order on the stairs, and it was coming towards the top, and it was coming towards the wall where Harry knelt.

It was coming for Evelyn.

She was beside him. She was on his left. She was kneeling with her hand on his back and her wand pointing down the steps at nothing, and she had not seen it, because nobody had. It was low and dark and the stairs were full of light and noise. It was ten feet away. It was five.

Harry shouted.

He shouted at it the way he had shouted at a snake once in the Great Hall, in his second year, with Justin Finch-Fletchley white in front of it. He did not decide to. He did not think about which language. He had never once in his life had to think about which language.

"*Leave her, get back, get b…*"

He heard it.

He heard the word change in his mouth.

He heard the hiss in it, the long cold hiss he had spoken all his life without hearing it, and he heard it, halfway through the word, become something else. It became a sound with corners. It became an ordinary English word, *back*, shouted by a boy on his knees on a stone stair with blood on his face.

The snake did not stop.

It did not hear him. He had said nothing to it. He had said *back*, in English, and snakes did not speak English. It came on, up the last step, with its mouth opening.

Millicent's Stunner hit it in the side of the head and threw it twenty feet down the steps into a knot of Death Eaters, and it lay there.

Harry stayed where he was.

He was on his knees on the top step. Evelyn was beside him. She had turned, and seen the snake go past her, and was looking at it, lying stunned at the bottom of the steps, and then she was looking at him.

He did not look back.

He was thinking about the word.

He was thinking about the place in his mouth where the hiss had been, and how it had felt to say it, which was like nothing, which was like speaking. He was thinking that he had never once heard himself do it. He had always heard English. He had heard *leave him* in the Great Hall in his second year and *open* at a sink in a bathroom and *Leave him alone* in a corridor, and every time it had been English to him, and every time it had been something else to everybody else.

He had just heard it be something else.

He had just heard it stop.

He knew what had come out of him. He knew what had been in him since a night in Godric's Hollow when he was one year old. He knew what he had been, all his life. He knew it the way you know, when you put your hand in your pocket, that the thing you have carried there for years is gone.

It was gone.

* * *

He looked down the steps at Voldemort.

He did not have to reach. He had never had to reach for anything less in his life. Voldemort was standing in front of the arch with his arms at his sides now, and his eyes were open, and he was very still, and he was not hiding anything. He did not need to.

Harry read him.

He had read him before. In a churchyard, enormous, a great cold pleased thing filling the night. Through his scar, in the dark, a house with a great many rooms. In December, along a thread, at the door of the cold. Every time, he had felt the same thing in it, under the size and the cold and the pleasure. He had never had a word for it. He had a word for it now, because it was gone.

There had been seams.

There had been places in the cold where it did not quite join. Where it had been split, and split again, and put back together badly, and the joins showed. Cracks. Gaps. The places where the pieces had been taken out and hidden in a locket and a ring and a cup and a crown and a snake and a boy. He had felt them every time without knowing what they were. They had been the only thing in the cold that was not cold.

They were gone.

The cold had no seams in it. It was whole. It was one thing, all the way through, from top to bottom, without a crack or a join or a gap anywhere, smooth and complete and enormous. It was smaller than it had been in the churchyard. That was the strange thing. It was smaller, and it was much, much worse.

Lord Voldemort looked up the steps at him, and smiled.

It was a small smile, and it was very pleased, and there was nothing behind it but the whole of him.

* * *

Sirius was by the arch.

Harry did not know how he had got there. He had been at the top of the stairs with the Order a moment ago, with his hand on Harry's arm. He had gone down. Somewhere in the noise, while Harry was on his knees with the cold coming out of his forehead, Sirius had gone down the steps after somebody, and he was at the bottom of the bowl now, on the edge of the dais, ten feet from the arch.

He was duelling Bellatrix Lestrange.

He was laughing.

So was she. She was laughing the way she had laughed out of the front page of the *Prophet* in January, private and enormous, and he was laughing back at her, his loud bark of a laugh, and they were going at each other on the edge of the dais with spells flying so fast that Harry could not follow them.

Harry had seen this.

He had seen it from the top of these same stairs, in another world, at thirteen, with Evelyn's hand in his. He had seen the other Sirius at the edge of this same dais, laughing, duelling this same woman. He had seen the other Sirius dodge one jet of light and laugh at her and call something out, mocking. He had seen her raise her wand again. He had seen the jet of red light hit him full in the chest.

He had seen him fall.

He had seen the look on his face. Surprise. Not fear. Surprise, and something like a question, as though he had not quite believed it. He had seen him fall backwards, very slowly, through the tattered veil, and the veil flutter as though in a high wind, and fall back into place.

Harry had kept that.

He had kept it for two years. He had kept it the way he kept everything he could not put down, at the bottom of him, with the other Sirius's love, which he had used to make a stag. He had kept the angle of Bellatrix's wand. He had kept the step Sirius had taken, to the right, laughing, just before. He had kept the colour of the light.

He was on his feet.

He did not remember getting up. He was standing at the top of the steps with blood on his face and nothing at the bottom of him any more, nothing cold, nothing pulling, and he was looking at Bellatrix, and he could see her.

She was a long way away. She was at the foot of the steps. He could not have reached her with a spell from here, not past forty Death Eaters and the Order on the stairs.

He did not need a spell.

He reached for her mind.

* * *

It was very easy.

It was easier than it had ever been. He did not know why. He did not stop to wonder. He reached across the whole of the Death Chamber, past everybody, and found Bellatrix Lestrange's mind at the bottom of it, bright and wild and delighted and full of Sirius, and he laid something over it.

He laid Sirius over it.

He laid him one step to the left.

It was very small. It was the smallest illusion he had ever made. He did not change anything about Sirius, not his face or his robes or his laugh. He only moved him, in her eyes, one step. Twelve inches to the left of where he really was. So that when she looked at him, when she raised her wand and aimed it at the centre of his chest, she would aim it at a man standing twelve inches away from the man who was there.

Sirius laughed. He dodged a jet of light. He called something out to her, mocking.

She raised her wand again.

Harry held the step.

The jet of red light came out of the end of her wand, very fast, and went across the dais, and it went exactly where the other one had gone two years ago in another world. Exactly. Full in the chest of a laughing man standing at the edge of a dais with his back to an arch.

It went through the illusion.

It went through the empty air twelve inches to the left of Sirius Black, and on, and into the black veil behind him, and the veil fluttered as though in a high wind, and fell back into place.

Sirius did not fall.

He was standing at the edge of the dais. He had stopped laughing. He was looking at the veil, which was still moving, very slightly, and then he was looking at Bellatrix, and then, quite slowly, he turned his head and looked up the steps at Harry.

Harry let go of the step.

He read Sirius look at him. Surprise. Not fear. Surprise, and something like a question.

And then something else.

* * *

Voldemort did not look round.

He was standing in front of the arch, whole, and he was looking up the steps at Harry. He had been looking at Harry since the snake. He had watched Harry get up off his knees. He had watched the jet of red light go through empty air into the veil. Harry read him watch it. He read the interest come up in him, cold and sharp, aimed at Harry.

And under the interest, something else.

It was curiosity. It was the curiosity of a man who has just put on a new coat and wants to see how it fits.

He raised his wand.

He did not aim it. He did not look where it was going. He raised it lazily, almost idly, out to one side, the way a man stretches his arm after sitting still for a long time, without taking his eyes off Harry at the top of the stairs. He was not trying to hit anybody. He was trying the wand. He was trying himself. He wanted to know what it felt like, now, to do this, whole.

A jet of green light came out of the end of it.

It went across the Death Chamber. It did not go towards Harry. It did not go towards Sirius, or Bellatrix, or the Order on the stairs. It went sideways, across the bottom of the bowl, at nothing, at the dim air beside the dais, where nobody was standing.

Somebody was standing there.

She had come down the steps after Sirius. Harry had not seen her go. Nobody had. She had come down the steps behind him to watch his back, because that was what she did, because she was an Auror and he was a fugitive with his face on every wall in the building, and she was standing a little behind him and to one side, with her wand up and her hair bright pink, at the edge of the dais.

The green light hit her.

She did not cry out. She did not have time. She was looking at Sirius, and then she was not looking at anything, and she fell. She fell sideways, onto the stone, at the foot of the arch, beside the place where the snake had been coiled.

She did not get up.

* * *

Harry read the place where she had been.

He did it before he knew he was doing it. He reached for her the way he had reached for her on a broom over London, and in a dining room with an apple on the table, and in a circular room an hour ago, grinning, with a cut on her cheek.

Happy. All the way down.

There was nothing.

It was not silence. Silence was Evelyn. Silence was a table with nothing on it, a room where somebody lived and had put everything away. This was the other thing. He had felt it in a churchyard, after a green light, where a boy called Cedric had been standing. It was the place where a person had been. It was the shape of her, exactly the shape, with nothing in it.

He went on reading it.

He did not know why. He stood at the top of the stairs with blood running into his eyes and read the empty place at the foot of the arch where Tonks had been happy all the way down, and he went on reading it, as though if he read it long enough something would come back into it.

Nothing came back into it.

Sirius was on his knees beside her.

Harry read him. He could not stop. He read him the way he had read Amos Diggory on a Quidditch pitch, a grief he had no word for, and he read, under it, something worse.

Sirius was looking at her. He was looking at the place where the green light had hit, and at the veil behind her, still moving very slightly, and at the empty air twelve inches to the left of where he had been standing. And he was thinking, Harry read it as plainly as though he had said it aloud, *it should have been me.*

*It was meant to be me.*

*Why wasn't it me?*

Harry did not know.

He would never know. He would think about it for a very long time. He would think about the twelve inches and the angle of Bellatrix's wand and the place where Tonks had been standing, a little behind and to one side. He would think about whether the green light would have gone the same way if a red one had not gone into the veil a second before. He would think about whether Voldemort had been aiming at nothing, or at something, or at Sirius, and missed.

He did not know.

Nobody did.

* * *

Voldemort lowered his wand.

He looked at the woman on the floor at the foot of the arch, briefly, without much interest, the way you look at the place where a stone you have thrown has landed. Then he looked back up the steps at Harry.

And then he walked away.

He stepped down off the dais, past Sirius on his knees, past the snake still lying stunned on the steps, past his Death Eaters, who drew back from him on both sides, and he walked across the floor of the chamber towards a door on the far side of the Death Chamber that Harry had not noticed. He walked unhurriedly. He did not look back. His Death Eaters fell in behind him, the ones who could still walk, Bellatrix first, laughing, and Lucius Malfoy with a face like paper, and a small round man with a silver hand who scuttled after the others like a rat.

The door on the far side of the chamber opened onto a corridor.

Harry knew where the corridor went. It went to the lifts. And the lifts went up.

To the Atrium.

"He's going up," said Harry.

Nobody heard him. Everybody was shouting. Moody was shouting and Lupin was going down the steps towards Sirius and Kingsley was calling for somebody to see to Ron. Neville was standing very still on the steps with his wand pointing at the door Bellatrix had gone through. Hermione had her hands over her mouth.

"He's going *up*," said Harry, louder. "He's going to the Atrium."

He was already moving.

* * *

He went out through the black door they had come in by, and through the circular room, and the doors did not turn for him, because he did not let them. He went out into the corridor with the black stone walls, and past the lifts, which were all going up, he could hear them, and to the stairs.

He took the stairs.

He took them two at a time. He did not know how many there were. Evelyn would know. There was blood in his eyes and on his robes and the split in his forehead was open and he could feel the air on it, cold, on the place where something had come out of him, and he did not stop.

He was running up the stairs of the Ministry of Magic after Lord Voldemort, and halfway up the third flight he noticed something.

Evelyn was beside him.

She was on his left. She had come out of the Death Chamber behind him and up the stairs after him, and she was running beside him, not quite level, a step behind, with her wand out and her face perfectly still. He had not heard her come. He had not looked round.

He knew she was there because she was silent.

He could feel the silence. It was beside him, on his left, steady and quiet and complete, the way it always was, a table with nothing on it. He had felt it every day for five years. He felt it now.

And behind him, coming up the stairs a flight below, he could feel the Order. He could feel them the way he always felt people. Lupin's grief, which was enormous and had no bottom. Kingsley's calm, under strain. Moody's fear, in every direction. Ron's pain and Hermione's horror and Neville's hate, which was like a lit fuse, and Millicent's anger, which was clean, and Luna's sorrow, which was very quiet and very kind.

Sirius was not with them. Sirius was still in the Death Chamber, on his knees.

Harry could feel him too.

He ran up the stairs.
